💡 Tips for a Watermark That Helps Instead of Hurts

A watermark should be noticed, not fought with. These small adjustments make a big difference:

  • Keep it light. A faint, semi-transparent mark reads clearly across a page without blocking the text you actually need.
  • Use capitals for short labels. Uppercase letters stand out against body text, especially in the middle of a page.
  • Try the diagonal position first. It is the easiest way to cover a page evenly without covering any single paragraph completely.
  • Match the wording to the purpose. A confidentiality mark and a "sample copy" mark mean different things to the person reading the file. Choose the one that actually applies.
  • Check before you send. Open the watermarked file on your own screen, at normal zoom, before it goes to anybody else.
  • Keep separate copies per audience. If the same document goes to several recipients, watermark each version with the recipient's name. It takes a minute and it makes tracking simple.
